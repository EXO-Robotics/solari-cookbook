# Proposed commercial licensing guide — not effective

**Current releases remain MIT.** The table below applies only if a future version explicitly includes approved, rights-cleared material under the final source-available license. It does not change earlier permissions or charge current users.

| Intended use of future covered material | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Read code, evaluate, run personally, modify for yourself | Free |
| Employer runs inspections for its own employees and internal operations | Free, even if the employer is a for-profit business |
| Employer uses a cloud VM/provider for its own internal deployment | Free under the AionGuard proposal; provider fees and terms still apply |
| Contractor installs or maintains the employer's internal deployment without selling hosted access | Free covered use; services/support boundary must be finalized |
| Redistribute covered material gratis under the same terms, outside a commercial bundle | Permitted with attribution, license, scope information, and modification notices |
| Sell copies, an OEM appliance, or redistribute the covered software for compensation | Written commercial agreement first |
| Run a hosted link-checking service for paying external customers | Written commercial agreement first |
| Include the covered code/client in a paid product or paid subscription, even without a separate software line item | Written commercial agreement first |
| Sell ordinary goods/services while using inspections only internally | Free internal-business use; no cut of unrelated revenue |
| Monetize only existing MIT versions | MIT permissions continue; no new fee or agreement created here |
| Independently implement the same idea without copying covered expression | This draft does not reserve the idea |

Hosting for your own employees is internal use; providing inspections to external subscribers is paid hosting. Calling the client free while making it a feature of a paid bundle would not avoid the proposed bundling requirement. Do not infer that any business activity requires payment.

The proposed commercial permission would be a **separate written, negotiated paid agreement** with identified, authorized rights holders. It should name the licensee, exact components/versions, permitted resale/hosting/bundling, duration, devices/customer scope, support responsibilities, and termination terms. Fees, revenue sharing, accounting, and any audit rights are negotiated separately. This review supplies no royalty rate, default debt, revenue definition, commercial signature, contact address, or assent mechanism.

This does not authorize Blake to contract for Megan’s rights or establish her agreement to relicense her contributions. A Solari commercial integration would also need applicable company/service agreements and any required brand permission.

Ask counsel and both contributors to settle: affiliates and contractors; consulting vs managed hosting; customer-facing internal portals; no-charge services funded by advertising/sponsors; freemium trials; donations and cost recovery; indirect compensation; promotional/free bundles accompanying paid products. These edge cases must not be resolved by silently expanding the definition of commercial use.

**Terminology:** call the commercially restricted additions source-available. MIT components remain open source. GPL and AGPL allow commercial use; copyleft/source-sharing obligations do not mean commercial users must pay Blake.
